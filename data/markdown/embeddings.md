\# Vector Embeddings and Similarity Search



An embedding is a way of representing text (a word, sentence, or document) as a list of numbers, called a vector, in a high-dimensional space. The key idea is that texts with similar meaning end up with vectors that are close together in that space, while unrelated texts end up far apart.



Embeddings are generated by neural network models trained specifically for this purpose, such as sentence-transformers models like all-MiniLM-L6-v2. These models are trained so that semantically similar sentences produce similar vectors, even if the exact words used are different.



To measure how similar two vectors are, cosine similarity is commonly used. It measures the angle between two vectors rather than their raw distance, which works well for text because it focuses on direction (meaning) rather than magnitude (length of text).



In a RAG system, both the documents and the user's query get converted into embeddings using the same model. The system then finds the document chunks whose embeddings are most similar to the query embedding, which is the basis of semantic search, a major improvement over old keyword-matching search since it can find relevant text even when the exact words don't match.
